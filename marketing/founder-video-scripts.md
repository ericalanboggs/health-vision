## 1. Ad / Landing video — "Why I built this"

*Direct to camera, ~75s. Phone-shot, no intro card, no music to start.*

**[0:00 — the scroll-stopper. Say it before anything else.]**
> "If your doctor told you to make some lifestyle changes — and that was pretty much the whole conversation — this is for you."

**[0:06 — who you are. Plain.]**
> "I'm Eric. Same thing happened to me."

**[0:10 — why the usual answer falls apart. No shame.]**
> "Every all-in plan I tried was gone by week three. Not because I'm lazy. Because there was no actual plan. Just do everything at once."

**[0:22 — the shift. The payoff — slow down here.]**
> "What works is smaller. Way smaller. One habit at a time — so small it's almost embarrassing. You run it till it sticks, then you add the next one. That's the whole thing."

**[0:38 — why you built it.]**
> "So I built the coach I went looking for and couldn't find. Direct. No shame. Still there on the days that get away from you — too slammed, slipped your mind, no time."

**[0:52 — the offer.]**
> "I put the first steps in a free guide. Made for men our age. Link's right here."

**[1:00 — the close. Small-step CTA, not a hype line.]**
> "Go grab it. Then go do the one small thing today."

**Delivery notes:**
- Carrying beats: **"Same thing happened to me"** (0:06) and **"so small it's almost embarrassing"** (0:22). Let both land with a beat of silence.
- Don't perform it. A half-smile on "almost embarrassing," a real pause on "not because I'm lazy."
- Dry aside on the "do everything at once" line is welcome; cut if forced.

**On-screen captions (sound-off autoplay):**
- 0:00 — `"Make some lifestyle changes." — every doctor, no instructions`
- 0:22 — `One habit at a time. So small it's almost embarrassing.`
- 0:52 — `Free guide → for men 40+`

## 2. Drip video — "Why I care"

*Direct to camera, ~60s. Audience already downloaded the guide, mid-nurture.
No hard CTA — day 13 makes the trial ask. Warmer and lower-key than the ad video.*

**[0:00 — open. Acknowledge where they are. No re-pitch.]**
> "You grabbed the guide a little while back. So you've already got the method — one small thing at a time. This isn't a pitch. I just wanted to tell you why I care whether it actually works for you."

**[0:12 — who.]**
> "I'm Eric. I built Summit."

**[0:16 — the honest reframe. The payoff beat — slow down.]**
> "Here's the thing. The guide works on its own. But knowing what to do was never the hard part. The follow-through is. The day you're slammed and it slips. The week that gets away from you."

**[0:32 — why Summit exists.]**
> "That's the whole reason I built Summit. Not an app barking orders. Someone in your corner on those exact days — a nudge, a quick adjust when life happens, a 'you didn't fail, just pick the next small thing.'"

**[0:48 — the personal close. No ask.]**
> "That's what I went looking for and couldn't find. So I made it. No ask today — I'm just glad you've got the guide, and I'm rooting for you. If you want someone in your corner for the part that's actually hard, that's what Summit's for."

**Delivery notes:**
- The hinge line: **"knowing what to do was never the hard part. The follow-through is."** Let it land — that's the guide-to-Summit reframe.
- Lower energy and warmer than the ad video. A guy checking in, not a hook. No CTA urgency.
- "the week that gets away from you" — busyness, not exhaustion.

# Postpartum — "Your Turn" founder video

day-10 postpartum drip email. Direct to camera, ~50–55s, phone-shot, warm. Voice: `ERIC_VOICE`

**[0:08 — who you are]**

**Corrected 2026-08-25.** This line previously read "Mayo-certified", which
breaches the credential guardrail in `marketing/STRATEGIC_DIRECTION.md` §7: the
credential reads "Mayo Clinic-trained", and no form of "certified" is used

**[0:16 — why you built it]**
> "I wanted something that takes health and breaks it into pieces small enough to actually fit a busy life — and makes sure a little time goes back to you."

**[0:26 — the surprise / moms]**
> "When I ran the early pilots, what surprised me was how much it landed with moms. Not a workout plan. Five or ten minutes on you, and someone actually in your corner."

**[0:36 — the science bridge]**
> "And it's not my hunch. It's how habits really form — start small, attach it to something you already do, one at a time. The experts have said it for years. They just never said it to someone running on no sleep with a kid on her hip."

- Carrying beats: **"lost track of yourself"** (0:00) and **"five or ten minutes on you"** (0:26). Let them land.
- "kid on her hip" is the line that proves you see the real day — keep it.
- The science bridge (0:36) is the credibility beat; say it plainly, no lecture.
- Open is adjustable — if "Hi moms" feels more like you, use it; just keep it warm, not cheery-salesy.

# `/plan` quiz segment cuts

*A separate family of videos from the funnel scripts above. These play on the
payoff screen of the pre-auth vision quiz (`/plan`, `src/pages/QuickPlan.jsx`),
one per acquisition segment, routed by `src/lib/quickPlan.js`. The burnout cut
also fills the video slot on `summit-web`'s burnout persona page; the other three
open by thanking the viewer for the quiz, so they are `/plan` only.*

*Not to be confused with the ad/landing video above (`1200892364`), which is
still what the persona pages and the day-10 drip use.*

**Shape all five share:** name the specific situation, introduce yourself, mark
that they named it, give an honest fork, refuse to resolve it on camera, land on
one small thing.

**Why the intro lands second, not first.** On `/plan` the video sits directly
under the vision statement the viewer built thirty seconds earlier, so opening on
their situation reads as continuous with what they just did. The bio goes right
after, which is where "who is this guy" becomes a live question. Keep it to two
sentences. Whatever the intro claims about credentials should sit comfortably
next to the wellness disclaimer the product carries everywhere else.

| Segment | Slug | Script |
|---|---|---|
| Burnout | `burnout` | Ready (below) |
| Postpartum | `postpartum` | Ready. **`/plan` only** — see the section |
| Warning signs | `lifestyle-changes` | Ready. **`/plan` only** — see the section |
| Not ready yet | `not-ready` | Probably unnecessary — the other cuts absorb it |
| Generic | `generic` | Ready. **`/plan` only** — see the section |

**Filming notes.** Phone, one take, sitting down. If it looks produced it stops
sounding true, and that's the whole advantage. Vimeo embeds are domain
restricted, so allowlist `summithealth.app`, `go.summithealth.app`, and
`localhost` or the player returns 401 and renders as an empty box.

## Burnout

~90 seconds.

> So you said burnout. Let's start there.
>
> I'm Eric, by the way. I built Summit Health, and I'm a health coach.
>
> First thing: you're in very large company. Burnout is everywhere in
> professional work right now. Most of the people carrying it are quiet about it,
> which is a lot of why it feels like it's just you.
>
> But you named it. That's the part I'd actually mark. Most people spend a couple
> of years calling it something else. Tired. Busy. A rough quarter.
>
> Now here's what neither of us knows yet.
>
> Some burnout is a boundaries problem. It needs a few new skills, some small
> changes, some practice protecting your own time. That version responds better
> and faster than people expect.
>
> And some burnout is information. It's telling you the work itself isn't lined
> up with the impact you want to have. That's also fine. It's a bigger change and
> it takes longer, and it's still a completely reasonable place to land.
>
> We're not sorting that out today.
>
> What we do over the next few weeks is change small things and watch what
> happens. What you learn from that is what tells you which one you're in.
>
> So we start small. One habit. Something that can survive a bad week.
>
> That's where we start.

**If you need it shorter:** cut "Tired. Busy. A rough quarter." That beat is the
ornament. The fork in the middle is load bearing.

**What changed from the raw version.** "It's awesome you identified it" became
"that's the part I'd actually mark" — the idea was right but "awesome" goes thin
in writing and reads as a pat on the head. "Aligned to the impact you want to
have" stayed, because it's the line only Eric would say and it puts the
vision-led product in one clause.

**Runtime.** Around two minutes, longer than the burnout cut. To get to ~90
seconds, cut the "every decade" paragraph and shorten the maybe-list to three

**Changed from the raw take.** "Thank you for taking steps toward a healthier
you" became a thank-you for the two specific things they actually did. The
original is the exact register `ERIC_VOICE.md` rules out, and naming the real

**Kept verbatim:** "What got us here won't keep us there." That one is Eric's and

**Say what you want on the sleep line.** Written as "gone to hell." The raw take
said "gone to shit," which is fine if that is the register wanted on camera.

**The fork here is readiness**, not the behaviour-vs-biology fork of the earlier

**Dropped from the earlier draft:** the "some of this is genetics or medication

after the maybe-list if the runtime allows.

## Postpartum

~2:45. Written for the **`/plan` payoff screen**, same as warning signs. Opens by
thanking them for the quiz, so it does not work on the persona page.

> Welcome to Summit.
>
> Before anything else: you just spent a few minutes being honest about where you
> are, and about how much time you actually have. That's not nothing, and I know
> time is the thing you have least of right now.
>
> I'm Eric. I founded Summit, and I'm a health coach.
>
> And let me say the obvious. I'm not a woman. I built this product, and I can't
> fully know what being postpartum is like. I'm not going to pretend otherwise.
>
> What I can tell you is that I've worked with postpartum women, and some of the
> realities of it line up with what Summit actually does.
>
> Maybe there's not much time that's yours. Maybe you haven't been able to put
> yourself anywhere near the top of the list. It's been the baby, it's been the
> family, and it's exhausting. And somewhere under that, you'd like to come back
> to yourself. Stronger. Calmer. The kind of person who has her own dreams again,
> and the confidence to go after them.
>
> I'll be honest, this wasn't who I expected Summit to work for. In the pilot,
> several women got more out of it than I anticipated, in two pretty different
> ways.
>
> One was deep in the overwhelm. Her life had shifted completely. What Summit gave
> her was time and space that was hers. Prompts to stop and think about what she
> actually needed and wanted, and reminders about the handful of things she'd told
> me mattered to her. A workout. Journaling. It wasn't complicated. It was that
> nothing else in her life was pointed at her, and this was. And I was in there
> too, chipping in, keeping some accountability going, cheering her on the way I
> do.
>
> Another was ready to dive back in. She was tracking habits again and getting
> herself set for a next pregnancy. Scheduling workouts, drinking enough water, a
> few other things she was moving on. What she needed was a system that kept it
> straight, and the reminders to carve out the time.
>
> Two very different places to be starting. Both fine.
>
> So if you want to pick one or two things and go, I'd love to help. I'll be here
> for it.
>
> And if you're not ready, that's fine too. Two things.
>
> One, Summit has a motivation mode. If you'd rather get inspired and learn for a
> while before you change anything, we'll send you that instead.
>
> Two, even if you close this today, you leave with a clearer picture of your
> vision and what it will practically take to get there. I hope that's useful on
> its own.
>
> Either way, we'll see you.

**Names removed.** The raw take named two real pilot users. Client stories in
public marketing need their explicit permission, and postpartum overwhelm is
more personal than a typical testimonial. Written unnamed with details
generalized. Get a yes before restoring names.

**Story order flipped.** The raw take led with the ready-to-dive-back-in story.
The overwhelmed one goes first here because that is the likelier state of whoever
is watching, and opening on "prepping for her next pregnancy" risks reading as
"not me." Flip back if that call is wrong.

**Bounce-back check.** The persona page promises "This isn't about bouncing
back." The dive-back-in story sits close to that line, so it is framed as her own
goal and followed immediately by "two very different places to be starting, both
fine."

**Claims check.** Both stories stay at what the women *did* — tracking, carving
out time, journaling, scheduling workouts. No health outcomes claimed, which
keeps this inside STRATEGIC_DIRECTION.md §7.

**Runtime.** Longest of the three. For ~2 minutes, cut the second story entirely
and keep "two very different places to be starting" as a single line. The
overwhelm story is the one doing the work.

**The disclosure is the strongest beat.** "I'm not a woman, and I'm not going to
pretend otherwise" does the same job as conceding a limit does in the other
scripts. Do not trim it for time.

## Generic

~2 minutes. The **`/plan`** cut for anyone who picked "Something else" on the
routing question, or arrived with no `?source=` tag. Currently that slot reuses
the old founder video (`1200892364`); this replaces it.

> Welcome to Summit.
>
> Before anything else: you just spent a few minutes being honest about where you
> are, and about how much time you actually have. That's the useful part, and most
> people skip it.
>
> I'm Eric. I founded Summit, and I'm a health coach.
>
> You didn't pick one of the boxes on that last question, and I'd guess that's
> because none of them were quite it. That's most people, honestly. Real life
> doesn't sort itself into three categories.
>
> Here's the thing though. Whatever brought you here, the parts that make a change
> actually stick are pretty consistent.
>
> You start smaller than feels satisfying. Small is what fits in a real day, and a
> real day is where this either works or it doesn't.
>
> You do one thing at a time, long enough that it stops taking effort. That's
> where momentum comes from. Not motivation.
>
> You attach the new thing to something already in your day, rather than to a good
> intention. There's a name for that, implementation intentions, and it's one of
> the more reliable findings in behavior change.
>
> And you don't do it alone. Support and some honest accountability change the
> odds more than most people expect.
>
> That's what Summit is. Not an app to obsess over. A coach in your messages, one
> or two habits at a time, and a check-in each week.
>
> I'm glad you're considering it, and I'd like to help.
>
> If you're not ready, that's fine too. Two things.
>
> One, Summit has a motivation mode. If you'd rather learn and get inspired for a
> while before you change anything, we'll send you that instead.
>
> Two, even if you close this today, you leave with a clearer picture of where
> you're headed and what it will take. I hope that's useful on its own.
>
> Either way, we'll see you.

**On "proven."** The raw take said these things are proven. No study is cited
here and no number is stated, per the standing rule about never asserting an
unverified stat. Instead the mechanism is named: *implementation intentions* is a
real, well-replicated construct in behavior-change research (Gollwitzer), and
naming it is the move `ERIC_VOICE.md` calls for — name it, nod to the evidence,
move on.

**The line to check before filming:** "Support and some honest accountability
change the odds more than most people expect." It is the softest claim in any of
the four scripts. It states no figure, but it does assert a direction. Either
keep it vague as written, or find a real citation and say the actual number
conservatively.

**Why this one is the least specific and should stay the shortest.** It has no
situation to name, so the recognition beat is replaced by naming the mechanics.
If it runs long, cut the "one thing at a time" paragraph. Start-small and
accountability are the two that carry it.

**Consequence for the video list:** with this written, `not-ready` almost
certainly does not need its own shoot. Every cut ends on the same two-option
branch, which handles it.
